# Uniforms and vehicles: what the figures rest on

The reference notes behind the low-poly people and vehicles in `src/people.js`, for Petrograd from October to December 1917. They were compiled from Wikimedia Commons, and every file named was opened and looked at.

The figures take their shapes from these notes. Their colours are close to the values below, not copies of them, and where Reed gives a colour his word wins: "the red shoulder-straps of the yunker-schools", the "elephant-coloured" armoured cars. Two touches are the map's own, and this file says why: the Red Guard's red arm band, and the machine-gun belts that some of the sailors wear crossed on the chest.

How to read this:
- Every claim names the Commons file it rests on.
- Almost all 1917 Petrograd photos are black and white. They support shape and light-or-dark tone, but not hue.
- Hex values marked **(sampled)** were averaged from a real colour image: an autochrome, a colour plate or a modern colour photo.
- Values marked **(assumed)** are conventional colours with no colour source found on Commons. Treat them as placeholders.
- A few references are not public domain (CC0 or CC BY). They are cited for colour only; none is shown on the map.

## Russian soldier, 1917 (Petrograd garrison)
- **Tunic (gymnastyorka):** a pull-over khaki shirt-tunic with standing collar, cloth shoulder straps and a brown leather belt. Khaki **#9c8a4a** lit, **#7c693b** shade (sampled). Source: `File:Soldats russes sur le parvis de la cathédrale - A11553.jpg` (Cuville autochrome, Reims, 5 Apr 1917, CC0). The French Adrian helmets and one pair of blue-grey trousers in that photo are French issue, so ignore them. The cut also shows in `File:WWI. РИА. 3-дм полевое орудие обр.1902 с расчетом у Ремерсгофа. 1916 war-15-01.jpg`.
- **Breeches:** khaki, **#7c693b** (sampled, same autochrome).
- **Boots:** black knee boots, **#27272f** (sampled, same autochrome).
- **Belt:** brown leather, **#635235** (sampled, same autochrome).
- **Greatcoat (shinel):** ankle-length, single-breasted, belted at the waist, with ammunition pouches on the belt. It is the main November silhouette: tall, long-skirted, belted. Grey-brown **#6e6556** (assumed). In black and white it reads mid-grey, clearly lighter than the boots. Sources: `File:At the gates of Smolny Institute 1.jpg`, `File:At the gates of Smolny Institute 2.jpg` (Smolny, Nov 1917).
- **Headgear:**
  - In November: a tall lambswool papakha, some light grey (**#8a8580**, assumed) and some dark (`File:At the gates of Smolny Institute 1.jpg`).
  - Otherwise: a soft peaked cap (furazhka) in khaki (`File:Maxim M1910.jpg`, `File:WWI. РИА. 3-дм полевое орудие…1916 war-15-01.jpg`).
- **Rolled greatcoat (skatka):** a fat ring worn over one shoulder and across the body. Source: `File:Maxim M1910.jpg` (soldiers hauling Maxims, 1916).
- **Cartridge bandolier across the chest:** worn by some men. Source: `File:At the gates of Smolny Institute 1.jpg`.
- **Footwear:** only knee boots are visible in the photos checked. Puttees (obmotki) were also issued in 1916–17, but none were seen clearly, so leave them optional.

## Junkers (officer cadets)
- **Field uniform:** the same khaki tunic, peaked cap, belt and knee boots as the soldiers. Source: `File:Юнкера в Петрограде 1917 г.jpg` (summer 1917, with Maxims).
- **Shoulder boards:** these show light edging, the junker braid, in that photo and in `File:Vladimir Junker Military School 1916.jpg`.
  - Strap colour varied by school. It is not verifiable from the black-and-white photos: use a bright accent (assumed red **#b0302a**) only if you need one.
- **November:** greatcoats like the soldiers' (no colour source).

## Women's Battalion (1st Petrograd Women's Battalion)
- **November dress:** belted soldiers' greatcoats, rifles slung, and fur hats.
  - The hats are mostly light-coloured: pale grey or white lambswool, **#cfcac0** (assumed).
  - Silhouette: same as the male soldier, slightly smaller.
  - Sources: `File:Batallón-muerte-rusia--russianbolshevik00rossuoft.png` (Palace Square before the General Staff arch), `File:2nd Moscow Women Death Corp Defending Winter Palace. St.Petersburg November 1917.jpg` (Palace Square by the Alexander Column).
- **Summer dress:** tunic, peaked cap and boots, with cropped hair. Source: `File:Женский батальон смерти. Петроград, июнь 1917.jpg`.
- **Officers with the battalion:** peaked caps with cockade, tunics with shoulder belts, and adjutants' aiguillettes (white or light cords on the right shoulder). Source: `File:Last guards of winter palace women batallion of death.JPG`.

## Baltic Fleet sailors (November)
- **Clothing:** black or very dark navy wool throughout: pea jacket or greatcoat, and trousers. **#1b1d24** (assumed). In black and white they are the darkest figures in any crowd.
  - Sources: `File:Marinerosrevolucionarios--throughrussianre00willuoft.jpg` (armed sailors on steps, winter) and `File:Red Guards checking documents.jpg`. The second has an uncertain date, but the dress is clear.
- **Cap:** peakless (beskozyrka), flat-topped, with a cap tally across the front. Sources: same two files.
  - Ribbons: black, with lettering (assumed gold).
- **Undershirt:** a striped telnyashka shows at the neck, white with dark stripes (assumed white **#e8e8e8** and dark blue **#27356b**). Source: `File:Red Guards checking documents.jpg`.
- **Weapons:** rifles with fixed bayonets. Source: `File:Marinerosrevolucionarios--throughrussianre00willuoft.jpg`.
- **Machine-gun belts crossed on the chest:** seen in the checked photos only on an armed man of uncertain service on the armoured car in `File:Броневик у Смольного 1917.JPG`. They are plausible for sailors but not shown on a sailor in these files.

## Red Guards
- **Clothing:** civilian workers' dress: a dark cloth cap (kartuz) with a small peak, a dark jacket or overcoat, sometimes a waistcoat and tie, and boots. A few wear soldiers' tunics or leather jackets.
  - Colours: charcoal **#2e2e30**, dark brown **#3b2f28**, grey **#5d5a55** (assumed).
  - Sources: `File:Red Guard Vulkan factory.jpg`, `File:Putilov factory combat team at shooting classes.jpg`, `File:Red guard unit Petrograd 1917.jpg` (leather jackets), `File:Red Guards patrol at the fire in the street.jpg`.
- **Weapons:** rifles slung or carried, sometimes with bayonets. Ammunition is in pouches or bandoliers over the coat. Maxim and Colt machine guns appear in `File:Red Guard Vulkan factory.jpg`.
- **Armbands:** a red armband is the usual Red Guard sign, but none is clearly readable in the photos checked.
  - `File:Red guard unit Petrograd 1917.jpg` shows small white tags or badges pinned to coats instead.
  - If you add an armband, use red **#b3262b** (assumed) on the left upper arm, and treat it as unverified.

## Cossacks (Don)
- **Trouser stripe (lampas):** red on dark-blue trousers. Don colours: navy **#1f2540** (plate sample #191c2e) and red **#b03a2e** (plate sample #ab4436) (sampled). The papakha top (shlyk) is also red (#a95144, sampled).
  - Source: `File:381 Changes in uniforms and armament of troops of the Russian Imperial army.jpg` (colour plate, 1870s dress). That dress is older than 1917 and only the colour scheme carries over.
- **1910s uniform:** dark tunics with shoulder straps, a peaked cap with a band (one man in a papakha), a curved shashka sabre without hand-guard, and boots. Source: `File:Казаки 4-го Донского Казачьего Полка.jpg` (photo of the 4th Don Cossack Regiment, which was in the 1917 Petrograd garrison).
- **In the field in 1917:** expect a khaki tunic over the dark-blue red-striped trousers (assumed; no 1917 colour source found).

## Officers
- **Uniform:** a tailored version of the soldier's uniform: khaki tunic or French-style jacket with breast pockets, Sam Browne-type shoulder belt, breeches, knee boots, and a peaked cap with cockade. Sources: `File:Last guards of winter palace women batallion of death.JPG`, `File:Vladimir Junker Military School 1916.jpg`.
  - Kerensky's own jacket (a high-collared tunic): `File:Alexander Kerensky 1917.jpg`, `File:Kerensky.jpg`.
- **Shoulder boards:** metallic gold or silver galloon, assumed **#c8a24a**.
- **Greatcoat:** a finer grey than the soldiers', assumed **#7f7d78** (no colour source).

## Townspeople
- **Workers:** caps, dark jackets and boots, as for the Red Guards. Women wear headscarves (white or patterned) and long dark coats. Source: `File:Manifestación-petrogrado-diciembre-1917--russianbolshevik00rossuoft.png` (women in headscarves marching in snow).
- **Bourgeois townspeople:** men in bowler hats and dark overcoats; women in long dresses or coats and wide hats.
  - Sources: `File:At the gates of Smolny Institute 1.jpg` (bowler hats, 1917) and `File:SPB Nevsky Prospekt from Police Bridge to City Duma, photochrome 1896-1897.jpg` (1890s street dress; photochrom colours are printer-added, not evidence of hue).
  - Overcoats black **#1e1e20** or dark brown **#3a2e27** (assumed); fur collars brown.

## Peasant delegates
- **Clothing:** long homespun coats (kaftan or armyak), belted or open; bast shoes (lapti) worn over foot wrappings (onuchi) bound with cords; long beards, staffs, and hats carried in the hand.
  - Colours: undyed grey-brown homespun **#7b6d5a**, light foot-cloths **#cfc6b0**, bast **#b89c64** (assumed).
  - Source: `File:CampesinosRusos--throughrussianre00willuoft.jpg` (A. R. Williams 1921, "Russian peasants").
- **Delegates who were soldiers** wear the soldier's uniform described above.

## Austin armoured car (Russian service, 1917)
- **Shape:** a boxy armoured body on a lorry chassis, with a long armoured bonnet and a louvred radiator front, spoked artillery-type wheels, and machine-gun turrets on the rear body.
  - `File:Ejército-rojo--russianbolshevik00rossuoft.png` shows a twin-turret car (Austin 2nd series per Commons) with men on its roof.
  - `File:Броневик и юнкера на Дворцовой площади 1917.jpg` shows a 1st-series car on Palace Square.
  - `File:Bolsheviks holding the end of a street with an armoured car.jpg` shows one at the Morskaya corner.
- **Colour:** dark olive green, **#3a4233** in shade and **#565b57** lit (sampled). Source: `File:Austin-Putilov armoured car - cental museum-01.jpg` (a modern replica at a reenactment, CC BY 4.0, so it shows a restorer's choice rather than a measured 1917 paint).
  - In the 1917 black-and-white photos the cars read as a uniform mid-dark tone.

## Petrograd electric tram, 1917
- **Shape:** a single-deck, four-wheel motor car with a raised clerestory roof, a trolley pole, end platforms and a destination board. Often it pulls a trailer.
  - Sources: `File:Petrograd. Soldiers on horseback and trams in Nevsky Prospekt. LCCN2011647896 (cropped).jpg` (a car on Nevsky in 1917), `File:MBTram-08.jpg` (Baltic Station, 1909), `File:Санкт-Петербург начала XX века. Садовая улица. Инженерный замок.jpg` (trams on Sadovaya).
- **Colour:** the period photos show a darker lower body and a lighter window band. The city's tram livery is red with cream: red **#c8402a** (lit patch #f2491e) and cream **#cec8bc** (sampled).
  - That livery was sampled from `File:SPbElectricTransportMuseum2008-04-15.jpg`, which shows later museum car types (1930s–60s). No colour image of a 1917 car was found, so treat the red and cream as an assumption for 1917.

## Cruiser Aurora (1917)
- **Shape:** a protected cruiser with three tall, evenly spaced funnels, two pole masts, a straight ram bow with anchors hung on the bow, guns along the sides, and a long low hull.
  - Sources: `File:Avrora1909-1914.jpg` (moored, 1909–14), `File:Avrora1916.jpg` (under way; despite its file name the description says about 1910).
- **Colour:** grey overall. The hull, upperworks, funnels and masts all read as one pale-to-mid grey in the 1909–14 photos (black and white).
  - The museum ship today is grey: **#8b8677** sampled under warm evening light. A neutral **#858a8d** is suggested. Source: `File:Крейсер Аврора (утро).jpg` (2008, CC BY-SA 1.0; a modern paint scheme).
  - The 1904–05 white peacetime livery mentioned on Commons for `File:Avrora1905.jpg` does not apply to 1917. Note: that file was not opened; only its Commons description was read.
- **Armament:** by 1917 it had been altered (1915–16 refits), so the deck guns differ in detail from the 1909–14 photos.
